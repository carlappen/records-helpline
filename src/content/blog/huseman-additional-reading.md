Early Reading from Jessica Huseman

*A lot of my ORR experience really kicked off with a 2022 training session with Jessica Huseman with The Friendly State News. She is incredible. She also gave me the OK to share some of the resources she gave us during that session. Here's a "Read First" guide she sent.*

---

### Recommended Readings on FOI

- **[Public Citizen's Guide to Filing a FOIA](https://www.citizen.org/our-work/litigation/litigation-how-file-foia-request)** — This is an advocacy group (so be aware of their agenda and goals) but this is still a very useful guide. **[SPJ offers a similar guide](https://www.spj.org/foi-guide-pros.asp)** for journalists specifically, but I do not find it to be as detailed.
- **[Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press's essay, "Entities Subject to the Law"](https://www.rcfp.org/browse-media-law-resources/digital-journalists-legal-guide/entities-subject-law-0)** — This is a useful guide for which government entities are subject to the federal FOIA, and which are not. My students, for example, often confuse non-profits and political parties with government entities. I point them to this guide frequently.

- **[Six Lessons from a Five Year Long FOIA Battle](https://www.cjr.org/first_person/foia_freedom_of_information_act_doj.php)** — This is a great first reading for students because it was written by someone new to filing records requests.

- **[LBJ's FOIA signing statement](http://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/ws/?pid=27700)** — This is a good, short read that helps students understand the mindset of this law — and maybe appreciate its history!

- **[FOIA tips from CJR](https://www.cjr.org/united_states_project/foia-expert-pro-tips-sunshine-week.php)** — This is a collection of nifty tips on FOIA requests from journalists who make a lot of them.

- **[FOIA is Broken: A Report](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/FINAL-FOIA-Report-January-2016.pdf)** — In 2016 the House Oversight Committee prepared this report breaking down all of the failures of FOIA. It features a few journalists. It is quite long, but you can find very useful snippets in here as examples of the problems journalists are likely to face as they make requests.

### Resources for Editors & Ongoing Learning

If you are an editor trying to replicate this training or support journalists as they continue to develop their FOIA knowledge, I find it is more educational to guide reporters to where they can answer their own questions about how to request a record (or where to request it from) rather than simply telling them:

- **[The United States Government Manual](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/publications/government-manual.html)** — This is quite literally a manual for the government. It lists all agencies, their roles, and their history. It is extremely useful for students who know very little about U.S. government structure, and even as a reference tool for more experienced journalists. If your student knows what record they want but not where to get it, send them here.

- **[RFCP's Open Government State Guides](https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-guide)** — This is the best place to refer students who want to make a state-level request. RFCP breaks down each state's laws, organized in such a way that makes it easy to compare by state. If your student wants to file a FOIL in a state, send them here first.

- **[Sample letters from the National Freedom of Information Coalition](http://www.nfoic.org/sample-foia-request-letters)** — They have letters for FOIA (which are not as specific to journalists as the one I've attached), and also for **[every single state](http://www.nfoic.org/state-sample-foia-request-letters)**. Students can use these guides to help them fill in their state FOIL letters.

- **[A list of FOIA offices for each agency](https://www.foia.gov/report-makerequest.html)** — If your student can't figure out who the records officer is for the agency they want to file a request with, send them here.

- **[The Office of Government Information Services](https://www.archives.gov/ogis)** — This is the agency tasked with ensuring FOIA compliance. It offers quite a few resources to the public. If things get dire, students can request assistance with federal records requests here. Some states have similar offices. For real FOI nerds, **[their blog is pretty good](https://foia.blogs.archives.gov/)**.

- **[MuckRock](https://www.muckrock.com/)** — This is an excellent tool for filing requests, especially in states that require you to be a resident of the state. If your student is unsure what language to use to request a specific record, tell them to search MuckRock for similar requests and use one as a model.

- **[The Art of Access](https://www.amazon.com/Art-Access-Strategies-Acquiring-Records/dp/1604265507)** — A great book, and would be a good textbook for an investigative journalism course with an emphasis on FOIA. It is a good general guide to records requests.
