It's certainly not necessary to always send your ORRs in the form of a letter. Agencies will often have portals where you can submit requests. A couple notes on those portals:

- You do not *have* to use them. Under state law you can transmit requests a number of ways, even over the phone. (NB: some elements of legal enforcement require written requests. As in, if you eventually have to sue the agency, you are likely going to need a record of that request. I suggest not relying on the phone call method. Plus, it's generally good to get things in writing.)
- If you have no issues using the portal, and the agency asks you to or indicates that's their preference, humor them. It's easier for folks to help you when they are not annoyed by you.
- Just remember that you do not have to transmit ORRs in any particular format, even if the agency says you do. (And some will.) If a city responds to your email and says you must use the portal, they are wrong.

Still, from time to time you may want to send a more formal letter — because they don't have a portal, or because a particular request calls for more *gravitas.*

Enter this letter template. You can also find it as a Google Doc [here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1cL-S7uDfz0GbNTH7kGmXKCA2TZ80vKL0bKOKctmnhug/edit?usp=sharing).

*This template originated with a version from [Jessica Huseman](https://www.thefriendlystatenews.com). There are quite a few other examples out there, such as [this one](https://law.georgia.gov/key-issues/open-government/how-make-open-records-request) on the state Attorney General's website.*

---

An ORR letter should include the following:

- **Your information.** Just a name and email is fine. I recommend a phone number as well.
- **The recipient of the request.** Ideally the specific records custodian (the City Clerk, for example), but when in doubt just use the agency's name.
- **The details of the request.** What you are requesting.
- **A reminder that Georgia law mandates a response within three business days and that any exemptions (including redactions) must be cited.**

**[DATE]**

**[Records Officer Name]**  
**[Agency / Department]**  
**[Street Address, City, State, ZIP]**  
**[Phone]**  
**[Email]**

**RECORDS REQUEST**

Dear Records Officer:

Under the Georgia Open Records Act § 50.18.70 et seq. I request access to:

**[Describe the records you are requesting. Be as specific as possible. You may also suggest search terms or methods to help the agency locate the records efficiently.]**

I would like to receive the information in the following format: electronic, and in machine-readable format. Preferably by email, but if this is not possible I will accept the material on a CD mailed to my address.

If there are any fees for searching or copying these records, please inform me if the cost will exceed $25. However, I would also like to request a waiver of all fees in that the disclosure of the requested information is in the public interest and will contribute significantly to the public's understanding of how the city strives for transparency in all government business. Please know that I am a member of the media, and this request is not being made for commercial purposes.

The Georgia Open Records Act requires a response time within three business days. If you expect a significant delay in responding to this request, please contact me with information about when I might expect copies or the ability to inspect the requested records.

If you deny any or all of this request, please cite each specific exemption you feel justifies the refusal to release the information and notify me of the appeal procedures available to me under the law.

Thank you for your assistance.

Sincerely,

**[Your Name]**<br>
**[Your Email]**<br>
**[Your Phone Number]**<br>
